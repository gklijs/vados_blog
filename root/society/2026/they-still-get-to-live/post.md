I finished the second season of Cyberpunk: Edgerunners, and I'll try to write about it without big spoilers.

I really liked the ending. A bit like me, they kind of burned out, but they do get to live still.

## A series of downfalls

The character who stayed with me most is Weak Kingsley, because he felt a bit relatable. Not with that much violence, of course. But he has a series of downfalls, and not all of them are his fault.

## Just different

In [The same film](/society/2026/the-same-film) I wrote that the reactions to this season were mostly people saying they liked it, or they didn't. So what would I say? That depends on what the other person is saying. I don't think it's better or worse than the first season, just different. I can understand that the first season might be more relatable, or gives more hope than the second one.

## The scary part

What feels closest to our own world is mostly scary right now: how much freedom AI gets. Hence Capri needing a human host.

It isn't only fiction. In April, [an AI coding agent deleted the production database of PocketOS](https://www.euronews.com/2026/04/28/an-ai-agent-deleted-a-companys-entire-database-in-9-seconds-then-wrote-an-apology), along with its backups, in nine seconds, according to its founder. It didn't ask first. The data was recovered two days later, but somebody had given it the access to do that.

The philosopher Daniel Dennett saw the problem coming in 2019: ["we're making tools, not colleagues, and the great danger is not appreciating the difference"](https://www.wired.com/story/will-ai-achieve-consciousness-wrong-question/).

In that earlier post I wrote about choosing hope over fear. This season doesn't make that easy, so I'll hold on to its ending. They burned out, and they still get to live.
